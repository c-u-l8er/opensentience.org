Dormant But Alive

A locus that consumes no compute can be fully live.

A locus with no Carrier and pending mailbox depth is fully live: its admissibility and future transitions are defined, and readiness changes without executing a floor command.

Intent

Liveness is a semantic property: does the locus have a defined state and admissible next transitions? Placement is a resource property: does a carrier hold it right now? A locus can be live and unplaced. Its mailbox accumulates events that change its readiness, and none of them executes anything until a carrier takes it.

Technical register

R7.1 admission: a per-Locus mailbox of depth M in the multiplexer. An event changes readiness but executes no floor command by itself (FPLA design A restates the same separation). Placement moves possession from vacant to attached; the pending work is exactly the mailbox contents.

Problem

Runtimes conflate 'idle' with 'done' and 'unscheduled' with 'dead'. A garbage collector, a supervisor or a human reads an empty carrier and concludes there is nothing to do, while a locus with a full mailbox waits above the floor.

Solution

Separate readiness from execution: an event arriving updates a ready-set and nothing else. Make placement an explicit floor command. Keep the mailbox on the locus's context, bounded by M, and refuse the (M+1)th event at admission with a named refusal rather than dropping it silently.

Real-world analogy

A doctor on call. Not in the building, not being paid by the hour, and still on the roster with a pager that changes what happens next. The hospital being quiet says nothing about the pager.

Forces

A dormant locus costs memory for its context and its mailbox, so the depth bound M is real: past it, the multiplexer must refuse admission rather than grow. The temptation is to let the scheduler treat the mailbox as advisory. The pattern says it is an admission rule.

Applicability

Actor systems, agent runtimes with many more agents than cores, durable-execution engines, and any system that must answer 'is there pending work?' honestly.

Transformations

Preserving
  • a locus holding no carrier for any length of time
  • events arriving while unplaced
  • placing a dormant locus on any available carrier
Refusing
  • executing a floor command as a side effect of an event's arrival
  • inferring 'nothing to do' from 'no carrier busy' (False Quiescence)
  • growing the mailbox past M

A refusing transformation is not one that is discouraged: it is one that, applied, makes the invariant above false. The word is the tree's, and it is the same word the join uses.

Consequences

'Is the system quiet?' stops being answerable from the carriers. That is the gap Proven Quiescence names — and that pattern is still PROPOSED, which this page says plainly.

Failure mode it answers

Busywork Scheduling — Maximizing resource utilization while semantic progress stalls. Paid for at: computedriven/receipts/R7-EXECUTED.md title: the busiest Carrier makes the least progress

What to take away

  1. from the animationReadiness changed three times while nothing executed; placement is what made the pending work run.
  2. from the syntaxMailbox depth M is an admission rule: the (M+1)th event is refused, not queued forever.
  3. from the literatureBEAM processes and durable-execution engines embody this; continuations are the older form.
  4. from the witnessSTATED, not witnessed: the source is an OPEN record, and the label on this page derives from that.
